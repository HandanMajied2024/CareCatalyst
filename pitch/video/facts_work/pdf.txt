Where Does Inequality Arise Along the Cancer Care Pathway?
Pohde
• Inequalities accumulate across the entire cancer pathway
– Prevention → screening → symptom recognition → access to care → effective
treatment → follow-up → rehabilitation / palliative care
• Socioeconomic status (SES) have a  role in cancer inequilities
– Income, assets and housing; education, occupation and employment; family
and residential circumstances have role in cancer inequilities
• Lower SES is associated with poorer cancer outcomes
– What we see;
-More advanced stage at diagnosis; less frequent receipt of curative or
systemic treatments, lower participation in screening and clinical trials
• Inequalities are often structural rather than voluntary
– Differences in access, resources and opportunities may arise beyond
individual choice
• Cancer risk also varies by socioeconomic group
– Lower SES: several smoking-related cancers; higher SES: some cancers
including breast cancer

Cancer Prevention
Pohde
Around half of cancer cases may be preventable
• Social gradient
Unhealthy lifestyle risks cluster in lower socioeconomic groups.
• Tobacco
A major preventable cause of cancer and premature mortality
•
>2000 new cases, 20% mortality with men >50y
• Diet and weight
Dietary patterns and overweight influence cancer risk; associations vary by
cancer type.
• Alcohol
Cancer risk rises with consumption; no risk-free level has been established.
•
No clear difference in consumption between SES groups
• Physical inactivity
Linked to increased risk of several cancers, including colorectal and
endometrial cancer.
• Prevention equity
Effective prevention requires policies and services that reach disadvantaged
populations.
